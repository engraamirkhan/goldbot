# Gold Trading System — Design

Oct 1, 2026 · Aamir Khan · exported from the Claude design doc; this file is the canonical copy going forward.

## Overview

The system is an evolving XAUUSD trading assistant that runs on a Windows VPS beside an MT5 terminal, trades the 15m and 1h timeframes with 4h, daily and weekly context, and retrains itself weekly under strict promotion gates. It executes through IC Markets and Vantage MT5 accounts, with Aamir confirming each entry from Telegram, and takes Aamir's TradingView indicators (via webhook and replication), scored economic news and TradingView chart ideas as inputs alongside price and macro data.

**Goals.** Positive expectancy after spread, commission, slippage and swap. Survive regime changes by switching weight between strategy specialists rather than relying on one model. Grow the training set from live data so the system improves with use. Run for weeks with Aamir confirming only trade entries from his phone, while sizing, stops, exits, reconciliation and retraining happen automatically under hard risk limits that no model output can override.

**Non-goals.** Discovering new data sources on its own: the model learns relationships within the features we build. Sub-minute or latency-sensitive trading: a CFD broker on a VPS is not the venue for it. Beating the market every week: the target is a small, durable edge compounded under tight risk.

**Constraints that shape the design.** The MetaTrader5 Python package is Windows-only, so execution lives on a Windows VPS while research runs on Aamir's Mac. TradingView indicators are closed-source, so they enter as logged alert outputs, not ported code. FCA retail leverage on gold is capped at 1:20 and overnight swap applies past 22:00 UK, so position sizing and the cost model must reflect both. Historical broker data runs to a few years at 1-minute resolution, so daily and weekly models need a deeper external history.

The sections below were drafted by four specialist agents (data, modelling, execution and infrastructure, red team) and reconciled into one design; where they disagreed, the more conservative option was taken and the alternative is listed under Open decisions.

## Data architecture

Two price sources, kept separate and tagged: Dukascopy's free tick archive (XAUUSD from May 2003) is the training backbone, and the brokers' own MT5 history is the execution-fidelity sample used for the spread, slippage and fill models. They are never blended; every signal must survive on both feeds or it is discarded.

**Historical.** Broker 1-minute bars come from `copy_rates_range` in monthly chunks on the VPS (set "Max bars in chart" to Unlimited first); realistic depth is one to three years. Until the VPS exists, the MT5 Mac app exports the same bars as CSV (View → Symbols → Bars) into `raw/mt5_export/`, read by the same loader. Dukascopy ticks are pulled with a scriptable fetcher (`dukascopy-node` or `tickterial`), keeping bid and ask as separate columns; 1-minute bars store bid OHLC, ask OHLC, tick count and mean spread. Paid data (Databento COMEX GC, history from 2010) is deferred until a feature needs real traded volume; CFD "volume" is tick count and is never labelled otherwise.

**Live.** One collector per broker polls `copy_ticks_from` every 250 ms, dedups on `(time_msc, bid, ask, flags)` and appends to the same tick store. Bars are built locally from ticks in both live and backtest so the two paths share code; a daily reconciliation against the broker's own M1 logs any divergence. Bar close is detected by clock (close time plus 1.5 s grace), not by tick arrival. Session state (daily break, weekend) comes from `symbol_info().session_deals`, not from silence, and no placeholder bars are ever written.

**Time and calendar.** Everything is stored in UTC with the broker's server offset alongside. MT5 timestamps are server-clock values labelled as UTC; the offset is resolved per bar via `zoneinfo('Europe/Athens')`, never hard-coded, because it shifts with EU DST. Resampling is `label='left', closed='left'`, a bar is complete only at `open + tf`, and bars that would straddle the daily break are not formed. Three "days" are named and never confused: the feature-day for daily bars and daily features ends at COMEX settlement, 13:30 America/New\_York (17:30 or 18:30 UTC depending on US DST); the swap-day is each broker's rollover time read from the terminal and used only for cost attribution; the risk-day for loss caps ends at 00:00 UTC. Weekly bars run Sunday open to Friday close. Both definitions live in a `calendar` table that the feature builder consults.

**Macro and fundamentals.** From [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/): `DTWEXBGS` (broad dollar), `DFII10` (10y real yield), `DGS10`, `DGS2`, `DFF`, `T10YIE`. [CFTC disaggregated COT](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/HistoricalCompressed/index.htm), contract code 088691, managed-money net and open interest; published Friday 15:30 ET for the Tuesday position. [GLD tonnes in trust](https://www.spdrgoldshares.com/usa/historical-data/), published the next business morning. Economic calendar: Forex Factory's free weekly feed archived daily by us, with Econoday or Trading Economics as the paid upgrade. Every macro table carries `value_date`, `available_utc` and `vintage`; features join with `merge_asof` on `available_utc` only, and a unit test seeds a fake release and asserts no bar sees it early.

**Economic news and headlines.** The calendar gives scheduled events; a separate news collector gives the unscheduled ones. Every 5 minutes it pulls gold- and macro-relevant headlines from free RSS feeds (ForexLive, FXStreet, Kitco, the Fed and BLS press pages) and the GDELT 2.0 event stream, stores each item with `published_utc` and `received_utc`, and scores it with a language model into a small fixed schema: relevance to gold (0–1), direction (hawkish/dovish, risk-on/risk-off, dollar-positive/negative), surprise (whether a release beat or missed consensus), and an "unscheduled shock" flag for events such as geopolitical escalations or emergency central-bank actions. Features are rolling counts and mean scores over 1 h, 4 h and 24 h plus minutes since the last high-relevance item; all join as-of on `received_utc`, so the model only ever sees what the system knew at the time. The RiskGate also reads this stream: a high-relevance unscheduled shock triggers a 30-minute entry blackout exactly as a tier-1 calendar event does. The dashboard shows the live headline feed with its scores beside the chart so Aamir sees what the model is reacting to.

**TradingView chart ideas.** TradingView publishes community ideas per symbol but offers no official API; a scheduled job every 30 minutes reads the public ideas pages for XAUUSD (directly or through a hosted scraper such as the [Apify TradingView ideas actor](https://apify.com/automation-lab/tradingview-community-ideas-strategy-scraper)) and records each idea's timestamp, author, follower count, declared direction (long/short/neutral), timeframe tag and title. These become crowd-positioning features: long/short ratio over 24 h and 7 d weighted by follower count, idea velocity versus its 30-day mean, and agreement between idea direction and Aamir's own indicator signals; extremes in crowd positioning are historically more useful as a contrarian or exhaustion feature than as a directional one, and the model is left to learn which. Two guardrails: the scraper is treated as a feed that can vanish (ToS or layout changes), so its features are NaN-safe and the system is tested with them blanked, exactly as for the webhook features; and ideas never enter as triggers. The dashboard shows the latest ideas as a feed, with direction tags, so they are also useful to read directly.

**TradingView signals.** Alerts fire "once per bar close" with a fixed JSON body: indicator, version, symbol, timeframe, `bar_time` (`{{time}}`), `fired_at` (`{{timenow}}`), signal, strength. FastAPI validates, stamps `received_utc`, and upserts on a content hash so retries are idempotent. Alerts with `fired_at − bar_time < tf` are flagged intrabar and excluded from training. Each indicator also gets a heartbeat alert, because an expired alert is indistinguishable from "no signal"; a missed alert sets the feature to NaN rather than a stale value, arrival latency is logged per alert in \`tv\_delivery\_stats\`, and training data has about 1% of alerts randomly blanked so the model has seen the NaN path before production. History is backfilled from TradingView's chart-data CSV export where the indicator plots values, tagged `origin='csv_backfill'`.

**Aamir's indicator suite (Knights of Gold), by ingestion route.** Each indicator on the chart is handled in one of three ways: replicated exactly in Python when it is a standard calculation with visible parameters, approximated and validated against TradingView's CSV plot export, or captured only through alerts when the logic is opaque. Replication is preferred wherever possible because it gives full history, no webhook dependency and no alert quota.

| Indicator on chart | What it shows | Route | Features produced | Alerts to configure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| KOG-MFI 2.0 (12) | Money-flow oscillator with 20/40/60/80 zones and dot signals at zone crosses | Replicate: standard MFI(12) on our bars, validated against the CSV plot | MFI level, zone (0–4), bars since last cross, slope | None needed; one "zone cross" alert kept for 90 days to confirm the replica matches |
| K-RB 2.1 (EMA ribbon, 1 / 15 / 300 params) | Five EMA values stacked as a trend ribbon | Replicate: fit EMA periods to the five exported plot series, then compute locally | Ribbon order (fully bullish, bearish, mixed), price distance to each band in ATR, ribbon width, bars since last flip | "Ribbon flip" alert kept for validation only |
| K-Indi 2.02: SMA/EMA 50 | 50-period moving average overlay | Replicate exactly | Distance to MA in ATR, slope | None |
| K-Indi 2.02: signal triangles and candle colouring | Proprietary buy/sell triangles (small and large) and candle state | Webhook: logic is opaque | One-hot triangle event by size and side, candle state, bars since last signal | Four alerts per timeframe: buy small, buy large, sell small, sell large, each once per bar close |
| K-Indi 2.02: red key levels | Horizontal support and resistance bands | CSV backfill for history plus webhook on touch | Distance to nearest level above and below in ATR, level age, touch count | "Price touches level" alert once per bar close |
| Trending? table (15, H1, H4, D) | Trend direction per timeframe | Approximate with our own ADX and EMA-alignment regime, validated against the table's CSV export | Already covered by the multi-timeframe trend features | None |

The chart feed is OANDA, not IC Markets or Vantage, so alert prices are reference only; alignment is by `bar_time`, and the few-cent feed difference is one more reason every TradingView-derived feature is a filter, not a trigger. Total webhook alerts needed: about 10 per timeframe for 15m and 1h, which fits a TradingView Essential plan on two timeframes and needs Plus if all five timeframes are alerted.

**Storage.** Parquet under `data/{table}/source=X/symbol=XAUUSD/year=YYYY/month=MM/` with ZSTD; DuckDB views over the globs for research. Tables: `ticks`, `bars_1m` … `bars_1w`, `macro`, `calendar`, `tv_signals`, `features` (by `feature_version`), `labels` (by `label_spec`), `trades`, `dq_events`. Live tables move to TimescaleDB only when concurrent writers or row-level alerting demand it.

**Quality checks on every ingest.** Gaps against the calendar, spikes (>12σ of trailing hour with no match on the other feed), duplicate timestamps, bid > ask, stale feed (90 s without a tick in session), non-monotonic time. Warnings commit with a `dq_flag`; errors quarantine the batch and alert. The trading loop never opens a position on a flagged bar.

## Features and labels

Features are built through a point-in-time store keyed on availability time, capped at 40 per live model from a candidate pool of about 200, and versioned so a model can only ever score the feature frame it was trained on.

**Feature families.** Returns and volatility (multi-lag log returns, ATR, Parkinson and Garman-Klass vol, realised-vol ratios). Trend (EMA stacks and slopes, ADX, Donchian position). Mean-reversion (z-score to VWAP and EMA, RSI, Bollinger %B). Breakout (range compression, distance to N-bar extremes, prior-session high/low breaks). Microstructure proxies (tick count, spread mean and max). Price structure (swing highs and lows with fractal lag, higher-high/lower-low sequences, gap from the daily break, body/wick ratios, inside and outside bars). Session and calendar (minutes since Asia/London/New York open, session flag, day of week, minutes to the next event by impact tier, DST flags for EU and US). Macro (level and 5/20-day change of each FRED series, managed-money net as a percentile of its 3-year range, GLD tonnage change). TradingView (one-hot signal, strength, bars since last flip, agreement count across indicators).

**Multi-timeframe rule.** For a decision bar opening at `t`, a higher-timeframe bar is visible only if `open_TF + TF <= t`. Implemented as a `merge_asof` on a `visible_at` column, and guarded by a test asserting that no 1h feature at 10:15 contains the 10:00–11:00 bar. The daily bar "for today" is not visible until the session closes.

**Labels.** Triple-barrier on mid price with spread charged at entry and exit (buy at ask, sell at bid), so a win must clear a full round-trip spread. Each specialist has its own barriers in ATR units of its decision timeframe; the starting values are set in the Modelling section and are subject to the ±25% grid in the research loop. The label is the sign of the first barrier touched, with the time barrier yielding the sign of the realised return. Meta-labelling from the start: the rule sets the side, the model learns P(rule is right). Overlapping outcomes are weighted by uniqueness (average inverse concurrency over the label's life) times absolute return; purging and a one-day embargo are mandatory in every fold.

**Leakage tests that run in CI.** A shuffle test (labels shifted by one bar must drive AUC to 0.5), an as-of audit on every join, and a Dukascopy cross-check: a signal that only works on broker-fed bars is treated as a feed artefact and dropped.

## Modelling

Four rule-triggered strategy specialists, each a LightGBM meta-labelling filter, sit under a regime allocator; nothing predicts raw direction on every bar. Direction prediction at 15m has near-zero signal-to-noise; rule-based candidates fix side and timing and leave the model the tractable question "is this instance of the setup worth taking, and how confident are we?"

**Specialists (starting parameters).**

| Specialist | Trigger | Target / stop (ATR) | Time limit | Exit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trend (1h primary, 15m execution) | 1h close on the EMA-50 side matching the 4h EMA slope, ADX(14) > 20, pullback to within 0.5 ATR of EMA then a 15m close back in trend | 2.5 / 1.25 of ATR(1h) | 48 h | Trail at 1.5 ATR once 1.25 ATR in profit |
| Mean-reversion (15m) | Close beyond 2σ of a 20-bar band, RSI(14) beyond 25/75, 1h realised vol in bottom two terciles | 1.0 / 1.5 of ATR(15m) | 12 bars | Barrier only |
| Breakout (15m/1h) | 1h range ≥ 8 bars and < 1.2 ATR wide resolves with a close outside; tick volume > 1.5× 20-bar median | 2.0 / 1.0 of ATR(1h), stop inside range | 24 h | Half at 1.0 ATR, rest trailed at 1.0 ATR |
| Session-open (15m) | London 08:00 or New York 13:30 UK: first two 15m bars agree, Asian range < 0.8 ATR(daily) | 1.5 / 1.0 of ATR(15m) | 16 bars | Barrier, hard flat 1 h before next session |

**Regime allocator.** Coarse inputs only: realised-vol tercile, 4h ADX bucket and daily EMA alignment, sign of 20-day change in real yields and DXY, days to next FOMC/NFP/CPI, session, day of week, and each specialist's trailing 60-trade out-of-sample hit rate versus expectation. It outputs a weight in \[0, 1\] per specialist with a hard gate at 0.2. To avoid leakage it trains only on stacked out-of-fold specialist predictions and outcomes, never on in-sample windows. For the first three months it is a rule table: trend 1.0 when ADX(14, 1h) > 25 else 0; mean-reversion 1.0 when ADX < 18 else 0; breakout 1.0 when 1h ATR is in its bottom quartile over 60 days else 0, with mean-reversion plus breakout capped at a summed weight of 1.0 since their conditions overlap; session-open always 0.75; everything 0 for 30 minutes either side of tier-1 events.

**From probability to position.** A candidate trades when calibrated p exceeds the specialist threshold and the allocator weight clears its gate, and only if expected value after costs is positive:

```latex
EV = p\,T - (1-p)\,S - c
```

where T and S are target and stop in ATR and c is round-trip spread plus modelled slippage. Size multiplier is a capped linear map, m = w · clip((p − p₀)/0.20, 0, 1), with p₀ = (S + c)/(T + S) the break-even probability. Probabilities are isotonic-calibrated on out-of-fold predictions; thresholds maximise out-of-sample profit factor net of costs, shaded up by 0.02, and are refit only at retraining.

**Validation.** Purged, embargoed walk-forward across all Dukascopy history, with windows set per timeframe: 15m specialists train 24 months, test 3, step 3, purge 2 days, embargo 1 day; 1h specialists train 36 months, test 6, step 6, purge 5 days, embargo 2 days. Purge is always at least the specialist's label horizon. Combinatorial purged CV (6 groups, 2 test, 15 paths) runs quarterly. Each specialist needs at least 1,500 labelled candidates overall and 60 per fold; it must show positive expectancy in at least three non-overlapping calendar years including the 2021–2022 chop, and pass a deflated Sharpe ratio of 0.95 or better using the real trial count from the research registry. Daily and weekly bars give too few setups to train on and remain context features only.

**Compute budget.** Learning from history is fast; proving it is slow. Gold since 2003 is about 530,000 15m bars and 135,000 1h bars, and each specialist trains only on its rule-triggered candidates (tens of thousands of rows, at most 40 features), so one LightGBM fit takes seconds and a full walk-forward for all four specialists plus the allocator runs in 20–40 minutes on a Mac. The monthly research loop is an overnight job; the weekly VPS retrain is minutes. The one-off Dukascopy download (100–200 million ticks, rate-limited) takes several hours to a day. The 18 months of paper and tiny-live in the roadmap are there because 400–600 trades are needed before a result is distinguishable from luck, not because training is slow.

**Retraining and promotion.** Every Saturday. Specialists use a rolling window matching their walk-forward train length (24 months at 15m, 36 at 1h); the allocator uses an expanding one. A retrained model is a challenger that shadow-trades at live prices for at least four weeks or 40 trades. Promotion requires shadow Sharpe within 0.5 of backtest and above 0.8 annualised, hit rate within one binomial standard error of expectation, shadow drawdown under 1.5× backtest, and turnover within 30% of the champion. Specialist promotion is automatic when all gates pass; allocator changes and any change to rules or label parameters need Aamir's approval. The previous champion is kept and restored automatically if the new one trips the CUSUM alarm in its first two weeks. Retired specialists keep running in shadow so the ensemble record has no survivorship bias.

**Drift and health.** Daily PSI per feature against the training distribution: above 0.1 on a top-10 SHAP feature warns, above 0.25 sizes down to 50%. Calibration drift by Brier score and reliability curve on the trailing 100 trades; expected calibration error above 0.08 sizes down. A one-sided CUSUM on trade-level PnL residuals (realised minus EV), tuned to a 5% quarterly false-alarm rate, halts the affected specialist; two specialists halted at once, or a 30-day drawdown above 1.5× backtest, halts the system pending review.

**Bounded research loop.** A monthly job with a fixed trial budget searches the \~200-feature pool, ±25% label grids and up to three rule perturbations per specialist. Every trial is written to a registry with a global counter that feeds the deflated-Sharpe correction, so the more we search the higher the bar. New specialists need a written rationale before entering the search space. This is bounded feature and parameter search, not discovery of novel strategies.

**Explainability.** TreeSHAP is stored for every live, shadow and rejected decision. The dashboard shows the top five features per trade with direction, calibrated p, EV and multiplier; rolling SHAP importance per specialist; and the PSI heatmap, reliability curve, CUSUM trace and allocator weights.

**Deferred.** No deep learning, reinforcement learning or per-bar direction models until twelve months of live data give an honest baseline to beat.

## Execution and risk

A `RiskGate` that no model output can override sits between the allocator and the broker; orders go to two portable MT5 terminals on one Windows VPS through a single `Broker` interface, with server-side stops attached at entry and idempotent client order ids so a restart can never double-fill.

**Broker abstraction.** The engine never imports `MetaTrader5` directly. A `Broker` protocol exposes `get_bars`, `stream_ticks`, `place_order`, `modify`, `close`, `positions`, `account` and `symbol_info`, returning engine-native dataclasses in canonical units. Because the [MetaTrader5 package](https://www.mql5.com/en/docs/python_metatrader5) binds one Python process to one terminal via `initialize(path, login, …)`, each broker gets its own portable terminal (`C:\MT5\ICMarkets`, `C:\MT5\Vantage`) and its own engine process on the same VPS, sharing clock, code and data. Symbol suffixes (`XAUUSD` vs `XAUUSD+` or `.r`), lot step, stop level, filling mode and digits are read from `symbol_info` at startup after `symbol_select`, never hard-coded; the engine refuses to start if a symbol is missing from Market Watch. Magic numbers encode account and specialist (IC 2601xx, Vantage 2602xx). A paper broker implements the same protocol on live ticks with fills modelled slightly worse than reality: entries at the touch plus half the last minute's spread standard deviation, stop fills with 20 points adverse slippage, real commission.

**Order lifecycle.** Market entries with SL and TP attached in the request, so protection exists server-side the instant the position opens; the engine layers trailing and time exits on top via `modify` and `close`, and the server stop is the floor. Deviation 30 points; filling mode preferred IOC, FOK, RETURN; retcodes 10004/10006 (requote/reject) retried twice with a fresh tick and 500 ms backoff, then logged `FAILED_EXEC` and alerted. Partial fills are kept at filled volume and never chased. Client order id `{account}-{bar_close_ts}-{intent_hash}` is written to a `pending_orders` table before `order_send` and stamped into the MT5 comment; on restart the engine reconciles that table against `history_deals_get` and never re-sends an id it has already seen.

**Sizing.** Risk per trade defaults to 0.5% of equity (0.1% in the tiny-live phase), scaled by the model multiplier bounded 0.25–1.5. Stop distance is 1.5 × ATR(14) on the decision timeframe, floored at the broker stop level plus spread. Lots = (equity × risk × m) ÷ (stop distance in $ × 100 oz), rounded down to `volume_step`; if rounding pushes risk above 1.2× target the trade is skipped. Combined exposure across both accounts is capped at 3 lots or 30% of combined equity notional, and treated as one position for all limits because two accounts on the same signal give double exposure and zero diversification. Each order is checked with `order_calc_margin` and against the FCA retail cap of [1:20 on gold](https://brokerchooser.com/safety/leverage-margin-limits-uk); it is rejected if projected margin level would fall below 300%.

**Hard limits.**

| Limit | Value | When tripped | Reset |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Per-trade risk | 1% max | Clamped, not rejected | n/a |
| Daily loss cap | 2% of own equity per account; supervisor cap 1.5% of combined equity | No new entries; open positions keep stops | 00:00 UTC (risk-day) |
| Weekly loss cap | 5% per account; supervisor cap 4% of combined equity | No new entries; owner told | Sunday 17:00 New York |
| Drawdown kill switch | Staged: 8% and 12% mark-to-market drawdown from the closed-equity high-water mark, per account and on combined equity | 8%: multiplier ceiling 0.5 and risk per trade halved. 12%: close everything at market immediately, no new entries | 8% clears when drawdown < 5%. 12%: /rearm with TOTP, then 10 trading days of positive paper shadow, then 30 days propose-and-approve |
| Open positions | 2 per account | Entry skipped | Automatic |
| News blackout | CPI, NFP, FOMC statement and presser, PCE: −15 to +30 min | No entries; early close if p < 0.5 | Automatic |
| Weekend | Friday 21:30 server | Close losers; tighten stops on winners | Automatic |
| Rollover | ±5 min of 00:00 server | No entries | Automatic |
| Max spread at entry | 45 points | Entry skipped, not queued | Automatic |
| Stale data | No tick 20 s in session, or last bar > 1 period old | No entries; alert | 60 s of healthy data |

**Supervisor.** A small third service reads both engines' heartbeat and equity files every loop, enforces the combined caps and combined drawdown stages above, and writes a `HALT` flag each engine checks every cycle. The combined caps are deliberately tighter than the per-account caps because both engines trade the same instrument and their losses are near-perfectly correlated. Engines fail closed: if the supervisor heartbeat is older than 60 seconds they stop entering.

**Account classifier.** The account type at each broker is not assumed; it is measured. On startup the engine reads `symbol_info` and `history_deals_get`: non-zero commission on past deals classifies the account Raw; zero commission with a median London/New York-session spread of $0.30 or more classifies it Standard; a median under $0.20 with floating spread and no deal history also reads Raw; anything in between is Unknown. The last classification is persisted and only changes when two consecutive runs disagree with it, so a restart during the Asian session never demotes a Raw account. On a Standard account the 15m specialists are disabled and the 1h and session-open specialists run only when expected edge exceeds 1.5× the measured cost; on Unknown the engine stays in paper and alerts. The classifier re-runs weekly from the same nightly cost job.

**Reconciliation.** On startup and every 30 seconds the engine compares its positions with `positions_get` filtered by magic. An orphan broker position is adopted, given a 1.5 ATR stop and reported; an engine record with no broker position is closed in the engine using `history_deals_get` for the true exit. Missing or out-of-range stops are reinstated immediately. Positions with unknown magic numbers (Aamir's manual trades) are left alone but listed.

**Operating mode: confirm the entry, automate everything else.** The standing mode is propose-and-approve for entries only. Each intent is sent to Telegram with account, direction, lots, stop, target, p, top three SHAP features and current spread, plus Approve and Reject buttons; the window is 90 seconds, timeouts are logged `EXPIRED_UNAPPROVED`, and approval re-runs the RiskGate and spread check at approval time. Once a trade is open the engine manages it without asking: server-side stop and target are attached at entry, trailing and time exits, partial closes, reconciliation, and every exit are automatic and are never gated by approval. Rejections carry a reason code (news, cost, discretion, duplicate, other) so the approve/reject log can show whether the human veto adds value. Paper mode runs for the six-month paper phase with the same Telegram flow so the habit is formed before money is at risk. A fully autonomous entry mode exists behind `/mode auto` plus TOTP and is only offered, never taken: after at least 100 proposals with no RiskGate breach and no statistically distinguishable difference between approved and rejected outcomes, the engine reports that the veto is no longer adding value and Aamir decides. A 12% kill-switch event always returns the system to propose-and-approve.

**Telegram rights.** Two owner-controlled accounts (main and a phone-only backup), accepted by numeric user-id allow-list. `/halt` and `/approve` need no second factor: halting is the safe direction and every approved trade is already bounded by the RiskGate. `/rearm`, `/mode` and any parameter change require a 6-digit TOTP within 60 seconds.

## Infrastructure and operations

Six Windows services on one London VPS: two trading engines, a supervisor that enforces the combined caps, the research scheduler, the webhook receiver, and a combined dashboard-and-alerts service, all under NSSM with the two MT5 terminals. Research and backtesting run on Aamir's Mac against a synced copy of the Parquet store. The services run 24/7: during the roughly 23 hours a day, five days a week that gold trades, the engines collect ticks, score bars and manage positions; in the daily break, overnight and at weekends the scheduler runs the nightly cost and quality jobs, the Saturday retrain, the research loop and the agents' reviews, so the system is never idle and never has to be started by hand. Watchdogs restart any service that stops, a heartbeat alert fires if any service has been silent for five minutes, and the Sunday maintenance window is the only planned downtime.

&#91;embedded content: system architecture · 5 services on one VPS, 2 terminals, 3 external feeds\]

External feeds enter at the top (indicator alerts, macro series, calendar, news headlines and chart ideas), the two engines sit behind one risk gate design with the supervisor enforcing combined limits, and each engine owns one terminal and one broker.

**Why NSSM, not Docker.** The MetaTrader5 package must talk to a terminal in the same Windows session, which Docker's isolation breaks. NSSM gives restart-on-failure, log rotation and start-on-boot with nothing else to maintain. Time is synced hourly with `w32tm` against a public NTP pool, and the engine logs terminal-versus-system clock drift; the stale-data check compares against broker server time, not local time.

**Dashboard.** A React + TypeScript single-page app (Vite, TanStack Query for data, lightweight charts for price and equity) served as static files, talking to a Python FastAPI backend over a typed REST API with an OpenAPI schema from which the TypeScript client is generated, plus a WebSocket channel for live ticks, positions and approvals. The backend is the only process that touches the store and the engines; the UI is stateless and works from any browser, desktop or phone. Authentication is multi-user: email, password and an authenticator code for every login, with three roles (owner can invite, remove, change roles, rearm and switch mode; approver can also confirm or reject entries; viewer reads only). The owner invites people with a single-use 72-hour link, each invitee enrols their own authenticator, five failed logins lock an account for 15 minutes, sessions are revocable, and every login, invite, role change and decision is written to an audit log. The dashboard is reachable from any device and browser without a VPN: a Cloudflare Tunnel on the VPS publishes the API over HTTPS, the tunnel token is entered once on the VPS, and the same backend serves the Telegram bot so an approval given in either place is reflected in the other instantly. It shows both equity curves, open positions with live P&L, today's and this week's P&L against caps, current probability and top SHAP features per timeframe, the last 50 trades, model version and last retrain, allocator weights, feed health (tick age, spread, terminal connected), the scored economic-news feed, the upcoming calendar with blackout windows marked, and the latest TradingView ideas for gold with their direction tags.

**Alerting.** Telegram for every fill and exit, any limit tripped, reconciliation mismatch, stale data, service restart, retrain result, and a daily summary at 22:05 server time. Email is reserved for the kill switch and failed backups.

**Logging, secrets, backups.** Structured JSON logs via `structlog` with rotation, plus SQLite tables for every decision (taken or rejected) with its SHAP values. Broker and Telegram credentials live in Windows Credential Manager through `keyring`; nothing in config files or environment variables. Broker logins are trading-only with no withdrawal rights and 2FA on both portals. Nightly encrypted `restic` snapshots of SQLite, trade log and model artefacts to Oracle Object Storage (S3-compatible endpoint, free tier; never GitHub), with a scripted weekly restore test.

**Security.** The webhook accepts POSTs only from [TradingView's published IPs](https://www.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000529348-how-to-configure-webhook-alerts/), verifies an HMAC over the payload in constant time, rejects replayed nonces, and answers within three seconds by queueing and returning. RDP is restricted to a Tailscale address, Windows Firewall is default-deny inbound except 443 and Tailscale, updates run in a Sunday maintenance window, and services run as a non-admin account. Telegram commands are accepted only from the two allow-listed owner user ids, and \`/rearm\`, \`/mode\` and parameter changes also require a TOTP, otherwise the approval channel is a spoofable kill switch.

**Running cost.** About $65–75 per month: a 4 GB Windows VPS in LD4 at roughly $27, a TradingView plan that supports webhooks (Essential at $14.95 covers it; Plus at $34.95 if more than 20 alerts are needed), and near-zero for backups and Telegram. Databento (COMEX GC) is not subscribed before month 9: after three months of paper, a one-off pull on the free credit (MBP-1, 1-minute OHLCV, daily volume and open interest) is tested, and the $199/month plan is taken only if futures volume or book-imbalance features improve any specialist's out-of-fold log-loss by more than 2% on a held-out year; the feature store reserves a \`comex\_\*\` namespace now so that is an addition, not a migration. Trading costs at both brokers' raw accounts come to about $0.12–0.20 per oz round trip including commission ($6–7 per lot) and typical spread, before slippage; long gold swap is around $12 per lot per night and triples on Wednesday. The RiskGate therefore enforces a minimum model-implied target of 2.5× round-trip cost, which at 15m means roughly 40 points of gross edge per trade. Costs are measured, not assumed: one nightly job per terminal fits a spread-by-session table from its own ticks, and a slippage table from the engine's own fills (requested versus filled, by session and order type), seeded with a conservative $0.15 prior until 50 fills exist. IC Markets' tables are the canonical ones for backtests; each engine gates entries against its own measured table at execution time, and backtests are reported under both.

## What the best systematic traders teach us

The firms with the longest profitable records win on discipline, risk architecture and execution rather than on exotic models, and the retail "AI trading" products that publish verifiable results are rule engines with walk-forward re-selection. This section is the evidence behind several design choices above.

**The institutional record.** Renaissance's Medallion is the only credibly documented outlier: 66% gross and 39% net annualised over 1988–2018 with no losing gross year ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/investing-expertise/proof-of-superior-investment-expertise/)), built on thousands of short-horizon statistical-arbitrage positions where the firm is "right 50.75 percent of the time" and never overrides the model ([notes on Zuckerman](https://novelinvestor.com/notes/the-man-who-solved-the-market-by-gregory-zuckerman/)); its public, longer-horizon sister funds are ordinary, which shows capacity and horizon matter as much as talent. By cumulative dollars the leaders are Citadel ($90bn), D.E. Shaw ($80bn) and Bridgewater ($79bn) ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/chris-hohns-tci-tops-hedge-fund-gains-2025)); their public lessons are about risk: Millennium winds down pods that lose 5–7% of allocated capital, Citadel refuses concentration in any single dimension and obsesses over the "last mile" of execution ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/awards/7960368/hedge-fund-of-the-year-citadel)). Two Sigma's $165m loss from unauthorised model edits ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Sigma)) is why every parameter change here is versioned and gated. XTX, with tens of thousands of GPUs, still warns that markets are "dominated by noise" and "AI is powerful enough to learn anything, including pure noise" ([XTX to Bank of Canada](https://www.banqueducanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Use-of-Artificial-Intelligence-in-Market-Making-XTX-Markets-020326.pdf)). Man AHL's deep momentum networks doubled Sharpe on 88 futures but only while costs stayed near 2–3 bp ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.04912)), and AHL paper-traded its first ML model for a year before deploying it ([Risk.net](https://www.risk.net/awards/5368836/quant-hedge-fund-of-the-year-man-ahl)). Winton cut trend-following before 2020, lost 21% and most of its assets ([FA-Mag](https://www.fa-mag.com/news/london-quant-pioneer-david-harding-gains-on-road-back-from--27-billion-plunge-71434.html)); Quantedge runs a declared 25% volatility target and tells investors to expect 10–40% drawdowns ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantedge)). Jane Street's $15bn July 2026 loss came from hedges that did not match a slow, diffuse loss path ([Gulf Times/Bloomberg](https://www.gulf-times.com/article/731123/business/jane-street-takes-15bn-hit-in-july-tied-to-ai-sell-off)).

**Retail AI assistants.** Trade Ideas' Holly re-runs a library of 70+ rule strategies nightly and deploys only those passing fixed criteria, which is a daily walk-forward selection loop rather than ML, and publishes no audited record ([Curved Trading](https://curvedtrading.com/articles/en/reviews/trade-ideas-review/)). Tickeron's pattern bots claim 40–169% annualised without independent verification ([Liberated Stock Trader](https://www.liberatedstocktrader.com/tickeron-review/)). Composer, Capitalise.ai and TrendSpider are rule engines with natural-language front ends ([Composer review](https://walnutinvest.com/resources/composer-review), [Capitalise.ai](https://support.capitalise.ai/en/articles/2164262-about-capitalise-ai)). QuantConnect rejected 80–90% of community alphas for look-ahead and unrealistic fills, found even the top 5% underperformed the S&P 500, and rebuilt around "hundreds of small, focused factors" with regime switching ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/13441)). A six-month production study of LLM trading agents found a 41% win rate against 50% for matched retail traders, volatility-blind leverage, herding, and that a fixed mechanical exit bracket beat the agents' own judgement ([arXiv 2609.05663](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05663)); HKU's live test of ten frontier models ranged from +10% to −15% in six weeks, with the most active traders doing worst ([HKU](https://www.hkubs.hku.hk/media/school-news/testing-ai-in-the-real-world-hku-business-school-released-ai-agents-trading-performance/)). The longest-running AI ETF, AIEQ, returned 20% over five years against 85% for the S&P 500 ([ETF Central](https://www.etfcentral.com/compare-etfs/AIEQ-vs-SPY)).

**What transfers to this system.** Edge is many weak signals aggregated at a 51–55% hit rate with controlled payoff, which is the meta-labelling ensemble. The signal is a minority of the result; spread gating, session filters, news blackouts and equity-state rules decide whether an edge survives live, which is the RiskGate. Volatility-targeted sizing and hard drawdown stops are the product, not a feature. Costs set the viable horizon, so holding periods are hours to days and slippage is modelled pessimistically. Walk-forward re-validation and a long paper phase are the retail-feasible version of AHL's year of proprietary paper trading. Regimes modulate sizing rather than flipping strategies wholesale, so the core edge is not abandoned when it sulks. Deterministic exits beat discretionary ones, so language models here explain, journal and flag anomalies but never decide exits. Trade-frequency limits follow from AIEQ's churn and HKU's finding. Effective leverage is capped far below the broker maximum, because leverage and liquidity, not bad models, are what have killed quants from LTCM onward ([Columbia Law](https://clsbluesky.law.columbia.edu/2018/09/10/a-retrospective-on-the-demise-of-long-term-capital-management)). The realistic solo-developer advantage is discipline: clean tick data, strict cost modelling, small size and a verifiable live record, which almost no retail AI product publishes.

## Evolving the system

The design is a starting point, not a fixed shape: every part that is likely to change is a plug-in behind a stable interface, every change is made by evidence through the same gates, and the system keeps the memory an experienced trader would, so it can get better at the job rather than only at the current strategy.

**Extension points built in from day one.** A specialist is a class implementing `candidates()`, `label_spec` and `exit_policy`, registered by name in config, so a fifth or tenth strategy is a new file, not a refactor. A feature is a named, versioned function in the feature registry with its own availability rule, so a new data source (a sentiment feed, order-flow data, a new macro series) is added without touching existing features. A broker is any implementation of the `Broker` protocol, so cTrader, Interactive Brokers or a futures account can be added beside MT5, and a second instrument (silver, DXY, GC futures) is a second symbol config over the same code. The allocator and the specialists are interchangeable model types: LightGBM today, but the interface takes any model that returns calibrated probabilities, so deep or sequence models can be trialled later through the same walk-forward and shadow process. Specialists, features and models are all versioned, and the trade log records which versions made each decision, so any past trade can be replayed under a new version.

**How the approach is allowed to change.** Anything can change, but nothing changes by opinion. A proposed change (new specialist, new feature family, a different label scheme, a new timeframe, a new instrument) is written into the research registry with a one-paragraph rationale, evaluated under the same purged walk-forward and deflated-Sharpe gates, shadow-traded beside the champion, and promoted only when it earns it. Quarterly, the system produces a review pack: what was tried, what was promoted, what was retired, and where the remaining edge appears to live by regime, session and specialist. That pack is where Aamir and the system decide the next quarter's direction together. The 40-feature cap and monthly trial budget are parameters, not laws; they rise as the live trade count grows, because more data buys more search.

**Demo first, live by gate.** All development, the Phase 2 paper period and every new specialist's shadow period run on IC Markets and Vantage MT5 demo accounts: the real broker feed, real symbol specifications and real server-side fills with no money at risk, which also lets the paper broker's fill model be checked against the demo account's actual fills. Live accounts exist in the registry from day one but are disabled; they can only be enabled after the paper-to-tiny-live gate is recorded as passed and Aamir types the exact confirmation phrase, and even then the engine refuses to return a live account unless the gate file agrees with the registry.

**Logins and credentials.** The account registry (`config/accounts.yaml`) holds everything except secrets: broker, demo or live, server, login number, terminal path, time zone, symbol, magic range. Passwords, the webhook secret and the Telegram token live only in the operating-system keyring (Keychain on the Mac, Credential Manager on the VPS). When the engine needs a credential it does not have, it asks Aamir at that moment, on the terminal if one is attached or by Telegram when running headless, stores the answer in the keyring, and never writes it to a file, a log or a chat. Adding an account is one command that prompts for login and password; switching between accounts needs no code change.

**No manual chores.** The operating principle is that nothing in the build or the running of the system should fall back on Aamir doing something by hand; his role is to confirm. Data downloads, broker history exports, VPS provisioning, terminal installs, service registration, backups and upgrades are all scripted in `ops/` and run by the assistant or by the app itself, each one surfacing a confirmation (Telegram or chat) before it acts. Two environment facts shape how that is done: the assistant's cloud workspace cannot reach market-data hosts, so data pulls and anything touching MT5 run on Aamir's Mac or the VPS, which the assistant drives through the linked desktop app; and MetaTrader's Python package needs Windows, so the VPS is provisioned first and then used for every broker-facing step.

**Source control and change flow.** The code lives in a private GitHub repository, `engraamirkhan/goldbot`, with `main` protected. Every change, whether from Aamir, the assistant or the engineer agent, arrives as a pull request; CI runs lint, the 24-unit test suite and a one-year synthetic dry run of the full pipeline on every push, and a change to a specialist, feature or label definition must also attach its registry trial id so the walk-forward evidence travels with the diff. The VPS deploys only tagged releases from `main`, and the running version is shown on the dashboard and stamped on every decision in the trade log, so any past trade can be traced to the exact code that made it.

**Account integrations.** MT5 accounts are first-class: any number of logins (IC Markets, Vantage, or others later) each get a portable terminal, an engine process and a magic-number range, and the supervisor treats them as one portfolio. TradingView is integrated in every way its platform allows: indicator alerts in by webhook, chart-data CSV export for history, chart ideas in by scheduled fetch, and, where Aamir's broker is one TradingView trades through directly (Vantage is), the dashboard deep-links each proposed trade to the matching TradingView chart so he can see it there before approving on Telegram. TradingView offers no third-party order API, so orders always go through MT5; the app never pretends otherwise. Any further platform (cTrader, Interactive Brokers, a futures FCM) is one more `Broker` implementation.

**Expansion across assets and styles.** Gold intraday on 15m and 1h is the proving ground, and expansion is gated on the live record, not the calendar: once gold has passed the full-size gate, the same code is pointed at silver (shares gold's macro drivers, so features transfer), then the dollar index and the major equity indices (US 500, Nasdaq 100, DAX, FTSE 100) as CFDs, each with its own session table, cost model, specialists re-validated from scratch, and its own paper phase. Styles widen the same way: scalping on 5m and 1m is unlocked only if the measured cost tables at a given broker leave room for it; swing and position trading on 4h and daily are unlocked as the trade count on those timeframes becomes large enough to validate, with swap costs modelled in; event-driven trading around releases is a separate specialist that trades the reaction, never the release itself. The allocator grows from weighting specialists on one instrument into a portfolio layer that weights every instrument-specialist pair under one volatility target and one drawdown budget, with correlation between positions counted so that long gold and long silver are not treated as independent bets.

**Scaling path.** Capital scales by raising risk per trade only after each gate, never by lowering thresholds. Instrument scope scales by adding correlated metals first (silver shares most of gold's drivers, so the macro features transfer), then uncorrelated markets if the framework proves itself. Data scales from Parquet and DuckDB to TimescaleDB when concurrent writers demand it, and compute from the Mac to a cloud GPU only if a sequence model earns a trial. None of these moves require rewriting the engine.

**What makes it an assistant, not just a bot.** The system keeps a structured journal: every candidate it saw, every decision it made or rejected with its SHAP reasons, Aamir's approve/reject choice and reason code, and the outcome. From that it produces a post-trade review after each exit (what the model expected, what happened, which features were right), a weekly report comparing Aamir's veto record with the model's, and a quarterly "what I have learned" summary of regime and specialist performance. A Telegram and dashboard chat interface lets Aamir ask questions in plain language over that journal and the live state ("why did you skip the London open today?", "how has the breakout specialist done in high-vol weeks?"), answered by a language model with read-only access to the store. Experience accumulates in the data and the registry rather than in one model's weights, which is what lets the approach change without losing what has been learned.

**Agents at the app's disposal.** The app has its own staff of language-model agents, each with a narrow role, scoped read-only tools over the store, and a budget; none can place, modify or close a trade, and anything they produce that would change behaviour goes through the research registry and the gates above. The roles mirror a small quant firm. A data steward runs the nightly quality report, investigates `dq_events`, and proposes loader fixes. A research analyst takes a hypothesis from Aamir or from the quarterly review pack, writes it into the registry with a rationale, runs the walk-forward and deflated-Sharpe evaluation, and reports the result in plain language. A macro and news analyst scores headlines, summarises the week ahead from the calendar, and writes the pre-session briefing. A risk officer reads the trade log and supervisor state each day and explains any limit that tripped, any divergence from backtest, and any concentration building up. An execution auditor compares fills with the cost model per broker and flags slippage drift. A journal coach writes the post-trade and weekly reviews and tracks whether Aamir's vetoes add value. An engineer agent drafts code changes as pull requests with tests against the specialist and feature interfaces; Aamir merges. An improvement agent runs continuously against three scorecards (expectancy after costs, hit rate against the model's own predicted probability, and precision of the entries it approved versus those it rejected), looks for where they are weakest by specialist, session, regime and feature, and files hypotheses into the registry for the research analyst to test: new support/resistance or gap definitions, different moving-average families or lengths, new pattern detectors, new data sources, changed barriers. It can propose anything; it can promote nothing, because every proposal still has to pass the walk-forward, deflated-Sharpe and shadow gates. That is how the system keeps learning without learning noise. Agents are invoked on a schedule (nightly, weekly, quarterly) or on demand from Telegram and the dashboard, every run is logged with its inputs, outputs and cost, and their total monthly spend is capped. The production evidence on LLM traders is clear that judgement at the point of execution loses to mechanical rules, so the agents' authority stops exactly where the RiskGate and the deterministic exit logic begin.

**A competing population of trading agents.** Every specialist variant is a trading agent with its own identity (`agent_id`, parent, generation, config hash), its own shadow and live track record, and its own capital allocation. Agents compete: the allocator gives capital in proportion to fitness, agents that stay profitable are cloned, and agents that lose are retired. This is how Millennium allocates to pods and how the research loop becomes a living population rather than a monthly job. The rules that keep the tournament honest are strict. Fitness is measured only on out-of-sample shadow and live trades at real costs (expectancy per trade, calibration of its own probabilities, drawdown, and correlation with the rest of the population), never on backtests, and an agent needs at least 60 shadow trades before it is ranked at all. Cloning means spawning two or three mutated children of a winner (perturbed barriers, a different feature subset within the 40-feature cap, a changed trigger threshold, a different timeframe) that start with zero capital in shadow and must earn their own 60 trades; a parent is never duplicated unchanged, because an identical clone adds exposure, not information. Retirement happens when an agent's lower 80% confidence bound on expectancy is below zero after 100 trades, or when it ranks in the bottom quarter of its generation for two consecutive months; retired agents keep running in shadow for six months so the record has no survivorship bias and a false retirement can be reversed. The population is capped (initially 12 live, 24 in shadow) and diversity is enforced by penalising fitness for return correlation with agents already funded, so the winners' children cannot herd into one trade. Total live risk never exceeds the RiskGate limits however many agents are funded, and promotion from shadow to live still passes the deflated-Sharpe gate with the population size as the trial count. Aamir sees the tournament on the dashboard as a league table with lineage, and the quarterly review pack reports which lineages are earning their keep.

**Long-term vision: a firm in software.** The end state is the functional equivalent of an experienced systematic trading and portfolio-management company: a data engineering function (the point-in-time store and stewards), a research function (specialists, the registry, the analyst agents), a risk function (RiskGate, supervisor, risk officer), an execution function (broker adapters, execution auditor), a portfolio-construction function (the allocator, extended from weighting specialists on one instrument to weighting strategies across instruments under one volatility target and one drawdown budget), and a governance function (versioned changes, gates, the quarterly review, Aamir as the investment committee). Each function already has its seat in the architecture, so growth is a matter of adding instruments, strategies and agents rather than redesigning. The firms that have actually compounded for decades did it with tiny per-trade edges, declared volatility targets, drawdowns they had planned for, and rules nobody overrides; that is the standard this system is built to, and "extremely profitable" is treated as an outcome to be earned trade by trade and proved on a live record, never as an assumption in the design.

## Risks and failure modes

The largest risk is the brief itself: "learn anything that will make it profitable" is an unbounded search, and an unbounded search over a noisy series always finds something, which is almost always noise. Every mitigation below constrains that search. If the system cannot be accepted as one that trades a few narrow, pre-registered hypotheses and ignores everything else, the project should stop after Phase 1.

**What "high probability" realistically means.** An honest intraday gold system has a hit rate of 40–55% with a payoff ratio of 1.2–1.8, giving 0.05–0.15R expectancy per trade after costs: an annualised Sharpe of roughly 0.8–1.5 if it works, with 8–15% monthly drawdowns at 1% risk per trade. A 70% hit rate at 1:1 payoff on 15m bars is leakage until proven otherwise. Separating a true Sharpe of 1.0 from zero at 95% confidence needs roughly 400–600 trades for a 0.1R edge; at 3–5 trades per week per specialist that is 18–36 months of paper and live data. The first year will produce no statistically meaningful verdict, and the design accepts that.

| Failure mode | How we detect it | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research-loop overfitting (thousands of trials on 300–800 events per specialist) | Deflated Sharpe with the registry's true trial count; a held-out year the loop never scores | 40-feature cap, fixed monthly trial budget, registry as sole source of N |
| Label and multi-timeframe leakage (daily bar visible before close, DST shifting the close) | Shuffle test collapses AUC to 0.5; as-of audit on every join | Point-in-time store keyed on availability time; purge and embargo |
| Specialist survivorship (allocator down-weights losers, record forgets them) | Ensemble backtest reported with allocator regret | Retired specialists stay in shadow |
| Regime non-stationarity (2024–26 one-way trend flatters trend models) | Per-year expectancy breakdown | Positive expectancy required in three separate years incl. 2021–22 |
| Backtest-vs-live gap (spread spikes to several dollars at rollover and events) | Spread and slippage per trade vs cost model | Spread modelled by time-of-day and event from our own tick log; swap on 4h leg |
| Broker-feed artefacts (model learns one broker's aggregation quirks) | Signal fails on Dukascopy | Every signal must survive both feeds |
| Terminal or VPS crash mid-position | Reconciliation on startup; chaos drill | Server-side stops at entry; idempotent order ids; NSSM restart |
| Symbol not in Market Watch after reboot (API returns None silently) | Startup assertion | Refuse to start until `symbol_select` succeeds |
| Weekend gaps of $20–40 blowing through stops | Gap log | Friday close of losers is a risk rule, not a strategy choice |
| TradingView alert expiry or revoked indicator access | Heartbeat alert per indicator | Missing-feature halt; system tested with all TradingView features zeroed |
| Two accounts on one signal | Combined exposure monitor | Treated as one position; each sized at half |
| Webhook spoofing or Telegram spoofing | Auth failures logged | HMAC + nonce + IP allowlist; sender-id verification |

**Regulatory and financial.** As an FCA retail client, gold is capped at 1:20 with 50% margin close-out and negative balance protection; an aggressive multiplier during a spread spike can hit close-out on both accounts at once, which is why margin level is held above 300%. Both brokers disclose that most retail CFD clients lose money ([IC Markets](https://icmarkets.eu/), [Vantage UK](https://www.vantagemarkets.co.uk/)). The UK tax difference between CFDs and spread betting is material and should be reviewed with an adviser before going live; this document does not give tax advice.

**The five mitigations that matter most, in order.** Constrain the search first: feature cap, trial budget, deflated-Sharpe gates from the registry's real N. Build every feature through the point-in-time store and prove it with shuffle and as-of tests. Model spread, swap and weekend gaps from our own tick log and require every signal to survive on Dukascopy as well as broker data. Make order submission idempotent and reconcile against the broker as the source of truth every cycle, treating both accounts as one exposure. Commit the trade-count and time gates, and the stop criterion, in writing before the first paper trade.
