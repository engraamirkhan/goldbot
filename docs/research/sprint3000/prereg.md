## 0. Integrity statement

- **No bar was read.** This lane did not open `raw/`, any Parquet file, any trade row or any shadow or exploration
  outcome. The brief allowed reading bars to count how often an entry fires. That option was not used, so the
  fire rates in section 4 are written guesses.
- **The ranking uses only these inputs:**
  1. The cost of record `cor-2026-10-31.2` (ICM), read through `ArmCosts.screen_charges`, plus `SwapSpec.shipped()`.
     Both are converted to basis points of price as W2 requires.
  2. The explore-window (2010-2017) gross numbers that earlier lanes already published (sprint-200 TREND, MEANREV,
     TIME, PATTERNS and MACRO, and R-06/R-10). These are listed in section 3.
  3. Fixed reference ATRs and the reference price level, stated below.
- **No confirm-window or holdout result, and no knowledge of gold's 2018-2025 price path, is used.**
  - Side is direction-neutral by construction. `long_only` and `short_only` have equal quotas (180 each).
  - Their only prior difference is the swap: longs pay it and shorts do not. That is cost arithmetic, not a view on
    direction.
- **No tuning after a look.** A changed spec is a new id and a new trial, as the brief says.
- **K:** every one of the 3000 specs counts in K, whether it fires or not. Program K goes from about 3,233 to about 6,233.

## 1. Spec format (contract with lanes ENGINE and KEYLEVELS)

Each spec has these fields: `{id, rank, prior_score, family, tf, entry:{type,params}, filters:[{type,params}], side,
stop:{atr_n, atr_mult}, exit:{type,params}, max_hold_bars}`.
- `id` is `S3K-<rank, 4 digits>`.
- `family` is a reporting group only. The engine does not use it. Its values are trend_channel, trend_ma, momentum,
  trend_onset, pullback, compression_breakout, level_break, key_level and session_breakout.
- Repeated blocks are stored once, as YAML anchors under `blocks:`. `yaml.safe_load` expands them, so every spec loads
  with the full schema. This keeps the file at 466 KB, under the repository's 800 KB limit.

The parameter names follow the brief's vocabulary. Where the brief gave no key name, this file fixes it:

| item | params |
|---|---|
| `session` filter | `{session: asia\|london\|ny}` |
| `weekday` filter | `{days: [0..4]}` (0 = Monday) |
| `inside_bar_break` | `{}` |
| `opposite_signal` exit | `{}` |
| `level_break`, `level_reject` | `{min_strength_pct, min_tf}`, with `min_tf` one of 15m/1h/4h/1d/1w (ADDENDUM A) |
| `near_level` filter | `{max_dist_atr, min_strength_pct}` |
| `room_to_level` filter | `{min_r}` |

Other conventions:
- A `time` exit has `max_hold_bars` equal to its `bars`.
- Other exits cap at 32 bars (15m), 48 (1h), 30 (4h) or 20 (1d).
- `atr_n` is 14, except 20 on 1d.
- `trend` filters use tf 4h only on 15m/1h entries. A 1d trend filter on a 1d entry is an EMA on the same timeframe.

If ENGINE has named a key differently, the orchestrator maps it in the engine's vocab layer. Changing the catalogue
instead would make a new catalogue version.

## 2. Cost arithmetic (Amendment W2)

The cost of record is in dollars at the 2026 price. W2 turns it into basis points of price by dividing by $4,000. For
the explore-window prior, it is priced back at a reference gold level of **$1,300** (the typical 2010-2017 level; a
price level, not a return), a factor of 0.325.

**Per-session charges** (ICM, round trip = max($0.23, ci90 upper); overshoot = max($0.12, ci90 upper)):

| session | round trip, 2026 $ | round trip, explore $ | overshoot, 2026 $ | overshoot, explore $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| asia | 0.272 | 0.088 | 0.136 | 0.044 |
| london | 0.230 | 0.075 | 0.120 | 0.039 |
| newyork | 0.230 | 0.075 | 0.196 | 0.064 |

- **Pooled** (the mean of the three cells) is used unless the spec pins a session. A spec pins one through a `session`
  filter, or through a session/opening-range entry, which is charged the session in which it fills.
- **Swap:** shipped long -$0.60/oz/night. That is 5.48% a year of notional, or **$0.195 a night** at $1,300. Shorts
  pay 0. Held nights are taken as hold hours / 23 x 1.4 (seven nights are charged per five rollovers). Long share is
  0.5 for `signal`, 1 for `long_only` and 0 for `short_only`.

**Reference ATR(14), 2010-2017:** $1.1 (15m), $2.6 (1h), $5.8 (4h) and $16.0 (1d). These come from the stop medians
the sprint-200 lanes published, not from a new measurement. Filters scale them as follows:

| filter | ATR factor |
|---|---|
| vol_regime 50-100 | x1.35 |
| vol_regime 0-50 | x0.7 |
| compression | x0.8 |
| atr_expansion entry | x1.1 |

**Expected exit behaviour** under a driftless walk (per-bar sigma = ATR / 1.25, m = stop multiple):

| exit | expected hold (bars) | stop-exit probability | sd of R |
|---|---|---|---|
| `target_r(r)` | 1.56 x r x m^2 | r / (1 + r) | sqrt(r) |
| `chandelier(c)` | 3.1 x m x c | 1 (a trail exit is a stop exit and pays overshoot) | 1.5 |
| `time(T)` | T | 2 x Phi(-1.25 m / sqrt(T)) | 1.0 |
| `opposite_signal` | half the cap | 0.45 | 1.5 |

Every hold is capped at `max_hold_bars`.

**cost_R** = (round trip + overshoot x P(stop exit) + swap) / (atr_mult x ATR_ref x filter factor).

W2 roughly divides the 2010-2017 cost per R by three compared with the dollar costs sprint-200 charged. That is the
main reason this catalogue ranks cost-sensitive 1h trend rules higher than sprint-200's results alone would suggest.

## 4. Prior score, diversity and composition

**Expected event count.**
- Formula: n_est = min(fire_rate x keep x explore_bars, explore_bars / (hold + 1)).
- Explore bars are 2,050 trading days x 92 / 23 / 6 / 1 bars a day (15m / 1h / 4h / 1d).
- Fire rates are per-bar guesses, set in `fire_rate()`. Examples:
  - donchian 1/n;
  - ma_cross 1.2/slow;
  - momentum_sign 1 (a state);
  - keltner/bb 0.06 / 0.025 / 0.01 at k = 1.5 / 2 / 2.5;
  - session and opening-range entries 0.7 a day;
  - level_break 0.02 / 0.012 / 0.006 at strength 50 / 75 / 90, times 1 / 0.7 / 0.5 / 0.3 for zones of 1h / 4h /
    1d / 1w.
- keep multiplies these factors:

| condition | keep factor |
|---|---|
| trend filter | 0.5 |
| mono side | 0.5 |
| vol_regime | its percentile width |
| adx_min | 0.5 |
| compression | 0.3 |
| session filter | 0.25 london, 0.3 ny |
| weekday | days / 5 |
| room_to_level | 0.6 at min_r 2, 0.4 at min_r 3 |
| near_level | 0.35 |

**Raw prior score** (an expected net t-statistic):

`raw = (g_prior - cost_R) / sd x sqrt(min(n_est, 1500))`

Penalties are applied to raw:
- -3 if n_est < 100: the spec cannot pass explore.
- -1 if 100 <= n_est < 150.
- -1.5 if n_est x 47/96 is below the TEST-OLD minimum (100 for 15m/1h, 60 for 4h/1d). This is W2: TEST-OLD is
  2006-02..2009-12, 47 of the explore window's 96 months. In practice it means a 1d spec must fire about 120+ times
  in 2010-2017, which pushes down 1d rules that fire rarely, as the coordinator asked.

**Candidate pool.**
- The pool has 544,320 specs: every grid point in `ENTRY_GRID` x tf x the filter sets x side x stop multiple x exit.
- Invalid combinations are removed: session entries only on 15m/1h; day/week/month levels only on suitable tf; zone
  tf >= entry tf; no chandelier exit on fades.
- Stop multiples by tf: 15m {2, 3, 4}, 1h/4h {1.5, 2, 3}, 1d {1, 1.5, 2}.

**Diversity (lazy greedy selection).** Each step takes the spec with the highest `raw - penalty`. The penalty, in t
units, grows with what is already chosen:

| already chosen with the same... | penalty per spec |
|---|---|
| family x tf | 0.004 |
| entry type and params x tf | 0.12 |
| entry + stop + exit (differs only in filters or side) | 0.25 |
| entry type | 0.001 |
| tf x stop multiple | 0.0015 |
| tf x exit type | 0.0015 |
| filter set | 0.0015 |
| mono side | 0.01 |

`prior_score` in the file is this adjusted value at selection. It never increases down the ranks (tested).

**Quotas.** The quotas sum to exactly 3000, so every pool is filled:

| pool | signal | long_only | short_only |
|---|---|---|---|
| main (grades A-C, no level primitive) | 2240 | 150 | 150 |
| level (level_break entry, or a near_level/room_to_level filter) | 280 | 30 | 30 |
| null controls (grade D: fades, retired session/inside-bar breakouts, level_reject) | 120 | 0 | 0 |

Caps: 15m <= 200 (the owner's scalping agenda stays covered at the same bar) and any one entry type <= 400.

**Null controls (120 specs).** These are retired ideas, kept on purpose as negative controls for the new engine. If
many of them pass explore, the engine or its control is leaking. That is what happened with the sprint-200 same-day
control.

## 7. Pass rules and reading rule (decided now, before any run)

**Pass rules.** These are the brief's rules as amended by W2, and they are not restated loosely here:
- Explore: n >= 100, net > 0, t >= 2.0 on day-summed R, and net minus leak-free control >= 0.
- Confirm: BH-FDR at q = 0.10 over m = the number of explore passers (m fixed before the run), plus net > 0, n >= 100
  and >= 5 of 8 years positive. DSR is reported at program K and at K_eff.
- TEST-OLD: n >= 100 (>= 60 for 4h/1d), net > 0, t >= 1.28, and net minus control >= 0.
- Qualified = all three.

**What to expect under the null.**
- About 2.3% of 3000 specs, roughly 70, will reach t >= 2 in explore by chance. Neighbouring specs are correlated, so
  that count will clump.
- Ahead of the run: **an explore pass count of 100 or fewer, concentrated in null controls or spread evenly across
  families, reads as noise.**
- **Continue** (the family is real) needs explore passers that cluster in the grade A/B trend families at 1h/4h
  **and** survive confirm BH-FDR **and** TEST-OLD.

**Stricter local reading rule (it tightens, never loosens, the shared rules):**
1. **Null controls.** If any of the 120 null controls (grade D) qualifies, no spec is deployed until the engine and
   its control have been audited. If more than 10% of null controls pass explore, the explore run is void and must be
   re-run after the fix, with every spec still counted in K.
2. **Clusters.** If qualifiers come from a single cluster of neighbours (same entry, tf and params), the cluster
   counts as one finding. Its rank-best member is deployed first, and its siblings stay shadow-only until the
   finding replicates in live shadow data.
3. **Stop.** If 0 specs qualify, the 1h-4h trigger family is closed at this cost level. The next step is a cost change,
   which is an owner decision (venue or swap-free instrument), not more triggers.

## 9. Known weaknesses of this prior

1. Fire rates and reference ATRs are guesses, made on purpose without looking at the data. Some specs will turn out to
   be n < 100. They still count in K.
2. The prior cannot separate parameter values within an entry type (for example the momentum lookbacks). Diversity
   spreads them; the screen decides between them.
3. The rank's tail is mostly filtered or mono-side variants of the same families. It runs and costs K like the head.
4. 1d has 398 specs. Under W2's TEST-OLD minimum, only frequent 1d rules (momentum states, short channels) are likely
   to have enough trades. Those are the ones ranked first.
5. The level primitives depend on lane KEYLEVELS. Until it merges, the 379 level-based specs cannot be evaluated.
   ENGINE must report them as "not evaluable" rather than drop them, and they still count in K.
